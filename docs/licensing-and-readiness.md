# Licensing and public-project readiness

Updated: 2026-11-17. The project owner selected MIT for project source code and original programming examples, and CC BY-SA 4.0 for original book prose and teaching diagrams. [README](../README.md), [LICENSE](../LICENSE) and [LICENSE-CONTENT.md](../LICENSE-CONTENT.md) state the intended scope. The [first-delivery ledger](delivery/milestone-1.md#6-release-readiness-remains-in-delivery-milestone-1) records the separately verified public reading release and its limits; license notices alone do not establish deployment or blanket rights clearance.

## Scope and exceptions

- **MIT:** project source code and original programming code examples, including programming snippets in book chapters, unless a specific notice says otherwise. Text diagrams and explanatory prose remain book content even in fenced blocks.
- **CC BY-SA 4.0:** original prose and teaching diagrams in every edition and language (`book/drafts/`, `book/revisions/`, `book/adaptations/` and the published site; scope widened 28 November 2026), to the extent the rights holder can license them. Attribution and share-alike conditions apply; commercial use is allowed. See [Creative Commons' official terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode).
- **Separately governed:** third-party dependencies, excerpts, images and other materials retain their own terms. Project names, logos and trademarks are not granted by these content notices. The selected licenses cannot grant rights not controlled by their licensors.
- **Other project documents:** internal planning and coordination records are not implicitly identified as book prose by this notice; clarify any desired additional scope separately.

## Contribution and publication checks

[CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md) explains how to report issues and propose changes. Contributors should have the rights necessary to offer their work under the relevant license and identify third-party material. This is not a claim that copyright in contributors' work transfers to the project owner, or that existing third-party works were relicensed.

Before integrating or publishing, check the actual work and asset inventory, provenance, applicable notices, code-example boundaries and attribution. Record unresolved rights rather than labeling unverified material as cleared. Preserve contributor credits and individual terms. If future commercial republication requires rights beyond a contribution's license, obtain the necessary permissions.

Create original prose, examples, visualizations and code rather than paraphrasing a source so closely that its protected expression or distinctive presentation is carried over. For actual quotations, verify necessity, applicable permission/exception, accurate wording and attribution; do not remove required attribution to hide a source. Keep a source/provenance trail for copied or adapted assets, externally sourced code and substantive AI-generated material, distinguishing ideas and verified facts from expression. A link alone is not a license. If a source or right is uncertain, identify it and resolve it before reuse; a name/phrase search cannot prove an entire project free of infringement.

For a later paid edition, review scope of CC BY-SA, co-author and contributor rights, third-party materials and cover/illustration rights before offering terms. Previously granted CC licenses retain their existing conditions for recipients; avoid suggesting buyers have exclusive access to already licensed expression or imposing incompatible downstream restrictions. Publication-platform AI disclosure and rights requirements must be checked against current official rules at the time of publication. This is an editorial and provenance process, not a legal-clearance or zero-risk claim.

CC BY-SA is a public license: a person who receives licensed material may retain its permissions under its terms even if the owner later changes the license for a new version. See the [Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/). A free reading site and any future paid service are separate product choices.

## Product focus and future identity

The author confirmed that near-term product Goals 1–3 focus on improving the existing book and its reading experience. The lightweight author/bookshelf portal belongs to Delivery Milestone 4, with design and a static build linking this book and actual later works. A simple entry needs no second completed guide; richer multi-guide work depends on a real second collection and reader need. A new umbrella brand or logo is a later decision, and formal publishing and broader commercial expansion remain deferred until after Delivery Milestone 4. The existing identity, truthful author information and ordinary collaboration opportunities remain available in the meantime. No new brand asset, business launch or advertising system is implied.
